# What we believe now (2026-11-25)

Do **N agents coordinated by mechanisms from nature** beat **N agents coordinated the way the market does it** (a manager agent, or a group chat) on work that needs N agents?

*Preliminary: synthetic code tasks. One model family for everything except the latency result at 160 modules (belief 7), which now holds on two families with one model each: Haiku 5.5 (LI38, replicated on fresh seeds in LI41) and `gpt-6-luna` (LI42).*

## Cyber (CC4, one model family, fixed reds)

*Preliminary: one simulator (CybORG CAGE 4), two scripted reds, Haiku 5.5, 30 seeds per red.*

| # | Belief | Confidence | Evidence | What would change it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **A simple swarm-style clearance rule defends CC4 well**: restore any host showing a suspicious process or connection, servers first, leave a restored host alone for 10 steps (`disinfect-cd10`). | Medium (CY0, CY1, CY1b have no independent review yet; CY2 does) | CY1b: beat `react` on 44 of 50 and 36 of 50 seeds; CY2: best arm under both reds | a red that adapts to the rule; the container range (R0) |
| C2 | **Telling an LLM team the rule helps it a lot, but it still does worse than the rule as code.** With the doctrine, LLM zone agents beat the plain LLM on 29 of 30 seeds under each red; the code rule beat the doctrine LLM on 30 of 30 (FSM) and 19 of 30 (Discovery), with fewer red Impacts. The LLM departed from the rule on about two thirds of its calls (Analyse and Remove instead of Restore). In CC4 the rule alone is enough; an LLM team adds cost, not defence. | Medium (one model, one prompt, no tuning) | CY2 (H1 supported; H2 not supported; H2' red-dependent), [US$ redacted] | a second model family or a prompt that keeps the LLM on the rule and then beats it; a setting with a wider choice than CC4's |
| C3 | **Three swarm-defence traits written as code add nothing over the clearance rule in CC4**: an alarm from other zones (SIG-04) hurts under one red; scaling the response to the threat (DEF-04) and a rotating sentinel (DEF-07) are not supported, and the sentinel ties exactly with the same number of random checks. All three shorten the cooldown, the direction CY1b found worse; in CC4 an alarm reports damage and does not warn. | Medium (screen level L0: code rules, untuned, two fixed reds) | GN-S3 (H-GN-S3a hurts under one red; H-GN-S3b, H-GN-S3c not supported), free | the R0 range, where red moves over several steps and an alarm can arrive in time |
