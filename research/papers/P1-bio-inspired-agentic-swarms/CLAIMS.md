## Coordination on real code, LI7 to LI11 (added 2026-11-17 after the internal audit)

These are the most direct evidence this paper has on "leaderless" coordination, and several go against it. All are local validation on synthetic executed code, one model family, not yet replicated on fresh seeds (method rules v3 in `docs/CONVENTIONS.md`).

| # | Claim | Level | Source | State |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Leaderless conversation does not finish coupled work: both sides of a format conflict adopt the other's format in the same round | local validation | LI7 to LI10; H-LI29 | ready with scope (0 of 68 codebases) |
| L2 | An arbiter on symmetric conflicts (one side holds the turn) lets a swarm finish | local validation | LI7, LI8; H-LI17, H-LI20, H-LI24 | ready with scope; "second model" is Sonnet to Haiku, both Claude |
| L3 | **A manager agent matched or beat the bio router at 5 to 80 modules and cost less** | local validation | LI9, LI10; H-LI25, H-LI26, H-LI28, H-LI31 refuted | must be reported; contradicts the leaderless framing at these sizes |
| L4 | One agent with every specification is as good as any team up to 80 modules when its reply fits | local validation | LI7, LI8, LI10; H-LI21, H-LI23, H-LI30 refuted | must be reported |
| L5 | Polarity (the requirement goes only to whoever can change the source) removes caller workarounds and beat the manager 15 to 11 of 16 | local validation, **oracle trace** | LI11 and review; H-LI33 (post hoc: not as worded), H-LI34 inconclusive | not ready: LI12 must show it without the oracle |

**Framing consequence.** The title "Towards Leaderless AI" and question P2 ("When do swarms beat an orchestrator?") are not supported by L3 and L4 at the sizes tested. Before any draft: either scope the paper to where a leader cannot hold the work (scale, latency, loss of members, open-ended tasks), or reframe it around which natural mechanism matches which conflict geometry, with the manager as the baseline it is.

## Claims the paper does not make

- That coordinating through a shared store with reinforcement and decay is new: ScaleMAS (2610.16120) does it, with an ablation of the citation weight, scaling to 128 agents and agent loss.
- That comparing a collective with one agent at equal budget is our standard: Fan et al. (2602.05289) define it. Ours fixes the unit to tokens, reports uncertainty and applies it to field-coordinated regimes.

- That swarms beat single agents in general.
- That any result on a synthetic counting or search task transfers to coding, research or defence work.
- That the biological mechanism is the reason an engineering mechanism works.
- Anything about the defensive use of the Wasps preset beyond instrument validation (`STATE.md`: no defence comparison is conclusive).
