## Main results, with cases

- **All three field wins are spec-violating helpers in the market arm.** Two are hidden workarounds by the caller that the agents' own runner could not report. The third is a helper that returns a constant, which only fresh inputs expose.
- **The field arm's real advantage is narrow.** Traces carried requirements to the helper's owner, so no caller worked around a helper. This fits the LI16 mechanism. The first case would most likely have gone to the market if the runner had shown the check it is scored on.
- **On efficiency the market wins:** fewer tokens, lower cost, fewer tool calls, and slightly shorter wall time.

## Skeptic's objections

- Only 3 pairs differ on quality, which is too few for any verdict.
- One model family, run through a subscription, with 4 teams sharing one rate limit (sweep 2 was also running).
- The tasks are synthetic, and some instances have test inputs that cannot tell a right helper from a wrong one (k40 s20406).
- The field arm's parent writes traces, so the arms differ in more than ownership.
- The wait behaviour can only be judged from what the agents wrote.
- The runner/scorer disagreement favours the field arm, because the field's rule forbids exactly the behaviour the runner cannot see.
