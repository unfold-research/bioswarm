## Is a replication with more cases the right next step?

Only as part of a redesign. Running the same design with 16 to 24 cases (about [US$ redacted] to 8 at these rates) would probably get the primary to p < 0.05, because the manager failed about half the cases. But it would confirm a result whose cause is already known: the manager's both-sides chase on symmetric checks. That result says nothing about trace-waiting or about scale.

A better next sweep, run in parallel under the same lockstep:

1. A manager arm that gets the same one-side symmetric tie-break, or that is told to assign one side per symmetric pair.
2. A stigwait arm without the tie-break.
3. At least two sizes (80 and 160) in the same sweep, so a scale interaction can be tested directly.
4. 16 or more cases.
5. Per-case tokens as the cost metric.

Only that design separates "the trace helps", "the tie-break helps" and "managers break down at scale".
