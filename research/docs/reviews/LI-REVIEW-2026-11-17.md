# Independent review of LI1 to LI3 (2026-11-17)

Two reviewers were run as separate agents on the preregistrations, instrument and raw data, and told not to read the author's reports: a methodology reviewer (Claude Opus 5.5) and a positioning reviewer (Claude Sonnet 5.5, with web search). Both are agents of the same lab run by the same research lead, so this is not external peer review.

## Methodology review: findings accepted

1. **`analyze-li.mjs` did not compute H-LI5, H-LI5b, H-LI6 or H-LI7** and printed LI1 rules under LI1 names for LI2 and LI3. Fixed the same day (rules per study, sign tests on paired final conflicts); `analysis.json` regenerated for all three studies.
2. **H-LI5 and H-LI5b are "supported" only degenerately** (0 = 0 solved, inhibit cheaper). On final conflicts the schedule result is the reverse: chat below inhibit in 21 of 24 cases (sign test p = 3e-4).
3. **The central agent is close to a no-op at the largest sizes**: at 192 schedule items it returned no plan in 91 of 96 calls (it answered that it could not build a verified schedule); at 240 colouring nodes 22 of 96 calls returned nothing. Starting it from the swarm's round 0 also hurts it: at 240 nodes `solo` beat `central` in 11 of 12 cases. "Swarm beats central at large n" is a win against a baseline that mostly declines.
4. **`patch` is not a swarm.** It averaged 0.98 calls per round (LI1) and 1.00 (LI2): with many conflicts the radius-1 groups merge into one component capped at 40 nodes, chosen in edge-list order, and that one member is told to fix conflicts whose nodes it cannot change. On schedules every item is linked to its whole team, so merging is total. What LI1 measured under `patch` is **one agent working a 40-node window around conflicts**, which ended below the whole-graph agent at 240 nodes in 12 of 12 cases.
5. **Inhibition barely reduces who acts** (7.5 against 9.2 members per round in LI1) and random activation does as well: what helps on colouring is fewer simultaneous moves, not the choice of side.
6. **The chat prompt differs beyond messaging**: 35 to 54% longer, a planner identity, a neighbour-owner list, schedule vocabulary on colouring, a two-field reply. 64 chat calls in LI2 truncated (2.5%), which biases against chat.
7. The latency proxy counts output tokens only.

## Positioning review: findings accepted

- Giving a conflict to one side is classical (distributed breakout, MGM, DSA); the narrow unestablished part is that LLM members with local territories inherit the oscillation and a classical activation rule removes it without messages.
- A single-author preprint (Rodriguez, arXiv:2601.08129, Jan 2026) already claims pressure-field coordination beats conversation-based coordination on meeting-room scheduling with 1 to 4 agents (48.5% against 12.6% solved). LI2 points the other way on a schedule with directed dependencies and 8 to 16 owners.
- "Coordinate through the field, not through chat" is not defensible as a general headline because the two domains disagree. Defensible: on symmetric constraints a classical arbitration rule beats free negotiation at lower cost; on directed dependencies messages carry direction the field lacks.
- Red flags: zero solves for every swarm arm; one small model; single-agent baselines win where the task fits; chat untuned; no classical non-LLM solver as reference; same lab preregisters, runs and reviews.
- Most credibility-raising follow-up: a second model family, a stronger chat protocol, and a classical solver reference, in one preregistered study.
