# 2606.30668 — Emergent Culture in Minimal LLM Systems

## 1. Citation as printed

Simon Jones and Sabine Hauert (University of Bristol, UK). "Emergent Culture in Minimal LLM Systems." arXiv:2606.30668v1 [cs.NE], 21 Jun 2026 (p. 1). ©2026 the authors, published under CC BY 4.0 (p. 1).

## 2. Task, models, agents, trials

- Task: none in the performance sense. Agents get the system prompt "You are a curious and creative agent. Explore and play!" and the user prompt "Cycle [n]. Continue your work." (p. 2). Output is free text.
- Models (Table 1, p. 2): claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929, kimi-k2-thinking-turbo, gemini-3-flash-preview, through LiteLLM.
- Agents: three per collective in the main experiment (p. 1, p. 2). Two combinations: Kimi-Kimi-Gemini (kkg) and Claude-Kimi-Gemini (ckg).
- Runs: main experiment 2 combinations × 5 repeats = 10 runs of 100 cycles (p. 2; ten run IDs in Table 3, p. 5). Pilot: all two-model combinations, 10 cycles, 10 runs, 20k-character budget (p. 2).
- Agents are stateless: no context carried between cycles; only the store and inbox persist (p. 2).

## 3. Mechanism and mapping

## 4. Baselines

- Single agent with the same total token budget: **No.** The only single-model comparison is anecdotal: prompting the same models over a full context window to write long-form text "failed" (refusals or loops), with no numbers (p. 7).
- Orchestrator / hierarchy: No.
- Non-bio schedule: No.
- No-decay control: **No.** The authors say so (p. 8).
- Reference set: human books from Project Gutenberg, "literature" vs "slushpile", for the recurrence analysis (p. 7, Fig. 6 p. 8). A shuffled-text null for the lag analysis (p. 7).
- Tokens / cost: no absolute figures. Only that Claude used 3.7× and 7.2× the tokens of Kimi and Gemini in the pilot (p. 2). Arms are not matched; the character budget was cut to curb Claude (p. 2).

## 5. Main results (as printed)

- Storage: about 100 keys after 100 cycles in kkg runs, over 150 in ckg; about 8 unique keys accessed per cycle in both (p. 4, Fig. 2 p. 3).
- Orientation behaviour: Claude and Kimi check inbox and list keys in 90.4% and 81.7% of cases, Gemini reads inbox 24.8% (p. 4).
- Three storage strategies from a PCA of three ratios (Table 3, p. 5; Fig. 4 p. 6): Accumulators (store rot 38–52%, discard 2–9%), Writers (59–97% of output to cycle-keyed names, rot 5–18%), Explorers (824 and 1,690 unique keys).
- Vocabulary: terms spanning ≥ 20 cycles, ≥ 10 uses, no gap > 10 cycles exist in the runs; proper nouns persist longest (p. 6, Table 4 p. 7). No counts or rates given.
- Output length: 75k–149k words per run (p. 6).
- Recurrence quantification (RR = 0.1, Theiler window 3, Lmin = 2, 500-point blocks, 100 bootstrap starts; pp. 6–7): swarm text has higher DET and ENTR than both human sets (Fig. 6 left, p. 8). No numeric table for this comparison.
- Long-range coherence, Table 5 (p. 8): integrated z-score of DETτ × ENTRτ beyond 15 cycles, against 100 shuffles, is positive in all ten runs, from 35.6 (run 154552) to 194.1 (run 161204). DET integrals 21.1–71.5; ENTR integrals 12.2–34.2; qualifying diagonals 53–133.

No confidence intervals or p-values are printed anywhere. The z-scores are per-run integrals, not tests across runs.

## 6. Ablations

None. Decay is always on, with one parameter setting. The authors: the design "does not isolate decay as the causal driver"; a sweep of corruption rates down to zero is named as the next experiment (p. 8). The system prompt was varied informally ("Research create play!"), reported without data (p. 8).

## 7. Scaling and robustness

Not reported. "How does the system work with more agents?" is listed as an open question (p. 8).

## 8. Claims vs evidence

- Abstract: decay introduces "evolutionary pressure" and agents show coherence "beyond the entropy horizon" (p. 1). The coherence measurement is supported by Table 5. The causal role of decay is not tested, as the authors admit (p. 8).
- "Reliably generate role allocation, coordination protocols" (p. 1): described qualitatively; no measure of role allocation is given.
- "We know of no systems that produce the length of output and maintain the coherence" (p. 8): no LLM baseline was run successfully (p. 7), so this is unsupported by comparison.
- Higher DET and ENTR than human books is presented as "qualitatively different", not as better; high determinism could equally indicate repetition. The paper does not separate the two.
- Internal inconsistency: the text names runs 154552 and 162025 as "Explorer" runs (p. 6, Table 4 caption p. 7), while Table 3 (p. 5) marks 155635 and 162025 as strategy 3 and 154552 as strategy 1.
- Small sample is acknowledged (p. 8).
