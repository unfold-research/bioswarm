# 2601.08129 — Pressure Fields and Temporal Decay

## 1. Citation as printed

Roland R. Rodriguez, Jr. (Independent Researcher). "Emergent Coordination in Multi-Agent Systems via Pressure Fields and Temporal Decay." arXiv:2601.08129v3 [cs.MA], 29 Jan 2026 (p. 1). Single author. "Use of AI Tools" declaration: Claude gave feedback on drafts (p. 50).

## 2. Task, models, agents, trials

- Task: meeting-room scheduling. Three difficulty levels: easy 3 rooms / 20 meetings / 70% pre-scheduled; medium 5 / 40 / 50%; hard 5 / 60 / 30% (Table 2, p. 23). 5 days, 30-minute slots, 20 regions of 2 hours. "Solved" = all meetings placed, zero attendee overlaps, within 50 ticks (p. 23).
- Models: qwen2.5 0.5b / 1.5b / 3b via Ollama, local RTX 4070 8GB (p. 24, p. 26, p. 51). Small models are a deliberate choice (p. 25). The claim that rankings would hold with frontier models is stated as a hypothesis, not tested (p. 25).
- Agents: 1, 2, 4 (p. 26, p. 56).
- Trials: 30 per configuration; main grid 5 strategies × 3 agent counts × 3 difficulties = 45 configurations × 30 = 1,350 trials, 270 per strategy (Table 3, p. 26; Table A2, p. 58). Seed = trial × 1000 + agent count, so all strategies see the same instance per trial (p. 55).
- Ablation: separate run, 30 trials per configuration, 2 agents, easy only (command on p. 56).

## 3. Mechanism (authors' terms) and mapping

"Pressure-field coordination": agents patch regions of a shared artefact; a hand-written pressure function scores each region; a tick has four phases — decay, proposal, validation, reinforcement (pp. 14, 18, Algorithm 1).

| Our category | Present? | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit of traces | Yes. Fitness/confidence boosts on patched regions; successful patches stored as few-shot examples ("positive pheromones"); rejected patches as "negative pheromones" | pp. 7, 14, 29–30 |
| Decay | Yes. Exponential decay of fitness and confidence; λf = 0.1, half-life 5 s; pheromone weight × 0.95 per tick, evicted below 0.1 | pp. 14, 24, 30, 42 |
| Threshold | Yes. Activation threshold τ_act on region pressure | pp. 14–15 |
| Quorum | No | — |
| Recognition / inhibition | Inhibition only: patched region is locked for τ_inh (2 s cooldown) | pp. 14, 24 |

Also present and not bio-specific: band escalation of sampling temperature and model escalation 0.5b → 1.5b → 3b after 21 stalled ticks (pp. 26, 31–32).

## 4. Baselines

Five arms, same models and prompt (p. 24, pp. 52–54):

- Pressure-field (ours).
- Conversation: "AutoGen-style" with Coordinator, Proposer, Validator; 4–12 LLM calls per tick (p. 54). The authors' own instantiation, not AutoGen itself (p. 54).
- Hierarchical: described on p. 24 as a "Single agent" that always picks the highest-pressure region, one validated patch per tick. Appendix p. 53 describes it as manager plus worker. Either way it is one sequential decision stream.
- Sequential: single agent, fixed order, no validation (p. 24).
- Random: random region, no validation (p. 24).

Explicit answers:

- Single agent with the same total token budget: **No.** The single-stream arms use 38K–46K tokens per trial against 617K for pressure-field (Table 11, p. 36). The fairness list matches ticks (50), models and prompts, not tokens (pp. 54–55).
- Orchestrator / hierarchy: Yes (hierarchical; conversation has a Coordinator role).
- Non-bio schedule: Yes (sequential round-robin, random).
- Tokens reported: Yes, per trial (Table 11, p. 36), per solve for two arms only (Table 12, p. 37), by outcome (Table 13, p. 37). Cost in money: not reported (local inference).
- Arms matched: **No.** Pressure-field uses about 3.8× the tokens of conversation and about 16× those of hierarchical.

The paper does contain a one-agent run of pressure-field itself (Table 6, p. 31). Token use by agent count is not reported, so it is not a matched-budget comparison either.

Ticks to solve, Table 9 (p. 35): pressure-field 17.8 / 34.6 / 32.3; conversation 29.4 (easy only); hierarchical 40.0 (n=4).

Tokens per solve, Table 12 (p. 37): 1.27M (pressure-field) vs 1.45M (conversation). By outcome, Table 13 (p. 37): pressure-field 318K solved / 900K unsolved; conversation 78K / 172K.

## 6. Ablations

Design: easy difficulty, 2 agents, 30 trials per configuration (p. 56; Table 5 caption p. 29).

Decay, Table 4 (p. 28): full 29/30 = 96.7% (CI 83.3–99.4%); without decay 26/30 = 86.7% (CI 70.3–94.7%). Fisher's exact p = 0.35. The authors say the difference is not significant and that there is a ceiling effect (p. 28).

Table 5 (p. 29): Full 96.7%; No Decay 86.7%; No Inhibition 96.7%; No Examples 90.0%; Baseline (all three off) 90.0%.

Fig. 2 (p. 29) adds three configurations: No Decay + No Inhibition 93.3%; No Decay + No Examples 83.3%; No Inhibition + No Examples 80.0%.

Printed contributions (p. 28, Fig. 3 p. 30): decay +10.0%, examples +6.7%, inhibition +0.0%. No test is reported for examples or inhibition.

Critical points the tables themselves show:

- With all three mechanisms off the system solves 90.0%, more than with only decay off (86.7%).
- Removing decay and inhibition together gives 93.3%, higher than removing decay alone (86.7%).
- So the effect of decay is not monotone across the eight cells, and every difference is 1–5 trials out of 30. The "+10%" is one contrast picked from a noisy grid.
- The ablation "Full" (29/30) and the main-grid easy 2-agent cell (28/30, Table 6) are the same configuration in separate runs; run-to-run spread is itself 1 trial.
- No decay ablation on medium or hard, where there is no ceiling.

## 7. Scaling and robustness

Table 6 (p. 31), pressure-field, easy, 30 trials each: 1 agent 25/30 = 83.3% (66.4–92.7%); 2 agents 28/30 = 93.3% (78.7–98.2%); 4 agents 25/30 = 83.3% (66.4–92.7%). The authors read this as "no significant agent-count effect" (p. 31). These three rows sum to 78/90, i.e. they are the easy cells of the main grid, not an independent experiment (compare Table 8).

One agent does as well as four on easy. Scaling by agent count on medium or hard is not shown. No fault-injection or robustness experiment is reported; fault tolerance is asserted (Table 1, p. 22; p. 42).

## 8. Claims vs evidence

- Abstract: 4× over conversation and 30× over hierarchical (48.5% vs 11.1% vs 1.5%), all p < 0.001 (p. 1). The numbers match Table 3. The comparison is not token-matched, and the hierarchical arm is degenerate (two thirds of runs never apply a patch, p. 39).
- "Implicit coordination outperforms explicit coordination" (p. 49). What is shown is that a parallel, validated, 617K-token search beats a 161K-token dialogue and a 38K-token greedy loop that gets stuck. Coordination style and budget are confounded.
- "Scales poorly with agent count" for hierarchies (p. 1) and O(1) overhead (Theorem 5.4): the only agent-count data is flat from 1 to 4 on easy (Table 6). No baseline scaling curve is shown.
- Decay: abstract says ablations "suggest temporal decay is beneficial" (p. 1); the body says not significant (pp. 3, 28, 49). The title and the section "why decay is necessary" (p. 20) rest on Theorem 5.3, not on data.
- "12% better token efficiency per successful solve" (p. 23, p. 37) is computed only against conversation.
- p. 42 says "3–30×", elsewhere "4×" and "30×".
- Protocol inconsistency: solved "within 50 ticks" (p. 23) but the appendix command for hard uses `--max-ticks 100` (p. 57).
- Convergence theorems assume separable pressure; separability holds here by construction because the overlap sensor ignores cross-block attendee conflicts (p. 24, p. 59).

## 10. Verdict for our claims

- **F1 — supported.** The collective is compared with conversation, a hierarchical/greedy stream and two schedules; no arm is one agent with the same total tokens, and Table 11 (p. 36) shows the arms differ by up to 16×. Table 6 (p. 31) further shows one agent matching four on easy.
- **F3 — supported.** One bio-inspired regime only; the other arms are non-bio (p. 24). The ablation removes parts of that one regime; it does not compare regimes.
- **F5 — supported.** Table 4 (p. 28): 29/30 vs 26/30, Fisher p = 0.35; Table 5 and Fig. 2 (p. 29) show the all-off baseline at 90.0% and no-decay-no-inhibition at 93.3%.
