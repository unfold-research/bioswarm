## 2. Tasks, models, architectures, configurations

- Six benchmarks: BrowseComp-Plus, Finance-Agent, PlanCraft, WorkBench, SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench (Table 1, p.9).
- Nine models: GPT-5-nano / 5-mini / 5; Gemini-2.0 Flash / 2.5 Flash / 2.5 Pro; Claude Sonnet 3.7 / 4 / 4.5 (p.12). Sonnet 3.7 is absent on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench, giving 8 models there (p.12).
- Five architectures: SAS, Independent, Centralized, Decentralized, Hybrid (Table 2, p.10; definitions p.7).
- Configurations: 4 benchmarks × 45 (9 models × 5 architectures) + 2 benchmarks × 40 (8 × 5) = 260 (p.10). The count 260 is right.
- Instances per configuration: 50–100 on the four main benchmarks, 20 on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench (p.10); exact sizes Finance Agent 50, BrowseComp-Plus 100, WorkBench 100, PlanCraft 100, SWE-bench 20 (seed 42 of 500), Terminal-Bench first 20 of 86 (p.41, E.4; p.27).
- Team size: 3 agents in every MAS (Independent 3 agents; Centralized 3 sub-agents + 1 orchestrator; Decentralized 3 agents) (p.41, E.2). A separate exploration goes to 9 agents on two Gemini models only (Fig. 5, p.23).
- No mention of repeated runs or seeds per configuration; variance comes from instances and bootstrap (Table 16, p.43).
- Inconsistency: the text says 8 models on SWE-bench/Terminal-Bench with OpenAI and Google families "unchanged" (p.12), but Table 16 (p.43) lists 9 rows per benchmark, including a `gemini-3-flash` row that is not among the nine study models. 9 × 5 × 2 = 90, not 80. The paper does not explain this.

## 3. How budget is matched (the paper contradicts itself)

Three different statements are printed:

1. Iterations: "we matched maximum total iterations between MAS and SAS systems" (p.12). MAS get "smaller per-agent iterations", SAS "proportionally more reasoning rounds" (p.12).
2. Tokens: "fixed computational budgets (matched total tokens across MAS and SAS)" (p.14); "All systems matched for total reasoning tokens (mean μ = 4,800 per trial)" (Table 5 caption, p.20); "matched for total reasoning-token budget (mean 4,800 tokens per trial)" (p.21).
3. Not matched: overhead is described as "1.6–6.2× token budgets relative to single-agent at matched performance" (p.18).

The data favour reading 3:

- Table 5 (p.20): turns are 7.2 (SAS), 11.4 (Independent), 26.1 (Decentralized), 27.7 (Centralized), 44.3 (Hybrid). Iterations are therefore not equal in practice.
- Table 5 (p.20): success per 1K tokens is 67.7 (SAS) vs 13.6 (Hybrid) while success rate is 0.466 vs 0.452. Equal token totals would give nearly equal success per 1K tokens; the printed ratio implies Hybrid used roughly five times the tokens. (The unit is also odd: 67.7 "successes per 1K tokens" cannot hold with 4,800 tokens per trial and a success rate below 1. My inference, not the paper's.)
- Appendix E.2 (p.41): SAS "maximum 10 iterations per instance"; Centralized up to 5 rounds × 3 sub-agents × 3 iterations; Decentralized 3 agents × 3 debate rounds × 3 iterations. The caps are 10 vs up to 45 vs 27 agent-iterations, which is not a matched iteration cap either.
- Fig. 3 (p.16) plots different dollar cost per experiment for each architecture.

Conclusion for our paper: this study cannot be cited as a clean "single agent with the same total token budget" comparison. It can be cited as "identical prompts, tools and a nominally matched compute budget", with the caveat that its own tables show unequal turns and token efficiency. The abstract says only "standardizing tools, prompts, and compute" (p.1).

- Values given only as whole percentages come from the annotations of Fig. 2 (p.11); they are not in the text.
- Overall mean MAS change: −0.3% (95% CI [−58.7%, +77.2%], σ = 37.5%) (p.14).
- Range: −70.0% (PlanCraft Independent) to +80.8% (Finance Centralized) (p.1, p.14).
- Mean success across all benchmarks (Table 5, p.20): SAS 0.466, Independent 0.370, Decentralized 0.477, Centralized 0.463, Hybrid 0.452. On average no architecture beats the single agent by more than 1.1 points.
- Overhead in turns (Table 5, p.20): 0%, 58%, 263%, 285%, 515% (SAS, Independent, Decentralized, Centralized, Hybrid).
- Trace-level error amplification (Table 5, p.20): 1.0, 17.2, 7.8, 4.4, 5.1 (same order). Its main effect is not significant: β = 0.014, p = 0.658 (Table 4, p.20; p.18).
- Success per 1K tokens (Table 5, p.20): 67.7, 42.4, 23.9, 21.5, 13.6.
- Turn scaling: T = 2.72 × (n + 0.5)^1.724, R² = 0.974 (p.21).
- Regression fit: R²_train = 0.463, R²_CV = 0.373 (±0.170 SD); with ACI R²_CV = 0.413 (±0.130) (p.17; Table 3 p.19; Table 13 p.42).
- Architecture selection: 87% correct on held-out configurations, vs 20% random and 54% capability-only (p.19).
- Capability threshold: P*_SA = −β4/β17 ≈ 0.040/0.236 = 0.170 standardized, "≈ 0.45 after denormalization" (p.19). Interaction P_SA × log(1+n_a): β = −0.236, 95% CI [−0.396, −0.076], p = 0.004 (Table 4, p.20). 94% match on 16 model×benchmark configurations (p.25).
- Out of sample (GPT-5.2, Gemini-3.0 Pro/Flash, BrowseComp-Plus): overall MAE 0.077 (15 predictions), MAS-only 0.061, SAS-only 0.138; 11/15 findings validated (Table 7, p.36). The equation predicts Hybrid as best for all three models and is wrong each time (p.37).

Critical notes on the 45% threshold:

- The numerator β4 (log(1+n_a)) is 0.040 with p = 0.487, marked non-significant (Table 4, p.20). The threshold is a ratio whose numerator is statistically indistinguishable from zero.
- Under cluster-robust inference the interaction `baseline_x_agents` is "Inflated", p = 0.105, and its Holm p is 0.084 (Tables 14 and 15, p.43). What survives both corrections is the main effect `single_agent_baseline` (naive p = 0.001, robust p = 0.004, Holm p = 0.018) (p.25, p.43). The paper calls this "capability saturation", but the surviving coefficient is the positive main effect of the baseline (β = 0.250), not the negative interaction that defines the threshold.
- WorkBench contradicts the rule: SAS 0.629 is above 0.45, yet Decentralized gains +5.6% (p.13); the paper itself lists "T = 16, P_SA = 0.63" as favouring decentralized MAS (p.19). PlanCraft and SWE-bench fit the rule; Finance and BrowseComp-Plus sit below it.
- The 94% figure rests on 20-instance cells with bootstrap CI widths of about ±20 points (p.26; Table 16 p.43).
- The coordination predictors in the regression (overhead, message density, redundancy, efficiency, error amplification) are "architecture-level constants … applied uniformly across all benchmarks" (Table 5 caption, p.20). They take five values, so they are close to a recoding of the architecture label.
- Internal slip: p.19 attributes 0.282 on planning to Decentralized; p.13 gives 0.282 to Centralized and 0.332 to Decentralized.
- Domain complexity D uses "empirically observed degradation under multi-agent coordination" as an input (p.38), which makes it partly circular as a predictor of MAS degradation.

## 5. Decentralised / peer-to-peer coordination

- Definition: all-to-all topology, consensus policy, agents talk in sequential debate rounds, no orchestrator (p.7; Table 2 p.10). Implemented as 3 agents × 3 debate rounds × 3 iterations per round (p.41).
- Highest mean success of any architecture: 0.477 vs SAS 0.466 (Table 5, p.20), at 263% turn overhead, 26.1 turns, error amplification 7.8, 23.9 successes per 1K tokens (Table 5, p.20).
- Best architecture on WorkBench (+5.6%) and BrowseComp-Plus (+9.2%); second on Finance (+74.5%); loses on PlanCraft (−41.5%), SWE-bench (−5.4%), Terminal-Bench (−6%) (p.13, p.14, Fig. 2 p.11).
- Coordination-failure rate 3.2% vs 1.8% Centralized and 12.4% Hybrid (p.23).
- Heterogeneous decentralized teams gain about +2.0 pp over the strong homogeneous team, while heterogeneous centralized teams lose 12.6 pp (p.26; Table 12 p.42).
- On held-out models the "Decentralized ≥ Centralized on BrowseComp-Plus" pattern does not hold for Gemini-3.0 Flash (0.48 centralized vs 0.40 decentralized) (Table 9, p.37).
- Shared-environment coordination is not tested. The only sentence on it: blackboard systems "assume structured message formats rather than natural language" (p.8). Every MAS here coordinates by explicit message passing (p.6). Nothing resembling trace deposit, decay, threshold, quorum or inhibition appears.

## 8. Stated limitations (pp.25–27)

(i) Canonical structures only; agent count explored up to nine, preliminary. (ii) Agents share base architecture; heterogeneity only preliminary (13 configurations). (iii) Tool-heavy environments are the main failure mode; no specialised protocol tested. (iv) Prompts identical across conditions but not optimised per model. (v) Six benchmarks; SWE-bench and Terminal-Bench use 20 instances, individual comparisons "underpowered at n = 20". (vi) Token cost and latency grow with agent count; coordination is serialised in natural language. (vii) Only G = 6 clusters; several predictors lose significance under cluster-robust inference; leave-one-dataset-out prediction of absolute success is weak.

## 10. What our introduction can safely write

Safe: "In a controlled comparison of 260 configurations across six agentic benchmarks, multi-agent architectures changed performance relative to a single agent by between +80.8% and −70.0% depending on the task, with a mean change of −0.3%" (p.1, p.14). Safe: "the multi-agent variants used 58% to 515% more turns than the single agent and achieved between 13.6 and 42.4 successes per thousand tokens against 67.7 for the single agent" (Table 5, p.20). Safe: "on a sequential planning benchmark every multi-agent variant lost between 39% and 70%" (p.13). Safe for our angle: "all four multi-agent architectures in that study coordinate through explicit messages or an orchestrator; coordination through a shared medium was not evaluated" (p.6, p.7, p.8). Write with a caveat: "the authors report diminishing returns once the single agent exceeds about 45% accuracy" (p.3, p.27), adding that the interaction term behind the threshold does not survive cluster-robust or Holm correction (Tables 14–15, p.43). Do not write that the study holds total tokens equal between single agent and collective: the paper states both iteration matching (p.12) and token matching (p.20), and its own Table 5 shows unequal turns and token efficiency.
