# 2509.11973 — MusicSwarm

## 1. Citation as printed

Markus J. Buehler (MIT, LAMM). "MusicSwarm: Biologically Inspired Intelligence for Music Composition." A Preprint. arXiv:2509.11973v1 [cs.AI], 15 Sep 2025 on the arXiv stamp; the title block is dated October 21, 2025 (p. 1). Single author.

## 2. Task, models, agents, trials

- Task: two-voice piano composition from a text objective. Objective #1: 8 bars in A harmonic minor with a given motif (p. 7). Objective #2: 16 bars "inspired by biological growth" (p. 13).
- Models: GPT-5-mini unless stated (p. 27); GPT-5 for the composing agents in Objective #2 (p. 13).
- Agents: one agent per bar (p. 27, p. 28), so 8 for Objective #1 (p. 7, Fig. 5 p. 10). For the 16-bar Objective #2 the Figure 11 caption says "eight agents" (p. 17) while the supplementary text speaks of "16 agents" (p. 45); the PDF is inconsistent.
- Iterations: global iterations 0–7 for Objective #1 (p. 7); 12 for the swarm in Objective #2, best at iteration 11 (Fig. 10, p. 16).
- Trials: one composition per system per objective is analysed. No seeds, no repeated runs, no sample size are reported for the comparison. For Objective #2 the author writes that the swarm gave "the best result by far" without a number (p. 13).

## 3. Mechanism and mapping

"Biological swarm intelligence system": bar-wise agents with personality vectors sense and deposit "musical pheromones" in a shared environment, assess neighbours' bars and form a consensus score; personalities are updated by an LLM step (pp. 6, 28–30, Algorithm 2).

| Our category | Present? | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit of traces | Yes. Pheromones with pattern type, strength in [0, 1], source bar, success score, timestamp | p. 29 |
| Decay | Yes, in the design: pheromones "undergo decay", weak ones are decayed each iteration, well-rated ones reinforced. No decay rate value is printed | pp. 6, 29, 30 |
| Threshold | Loose. Themes are "detected when strong, repeated patterns occur"; no numeric threshold given | p. 29 |
| Quorum | No. "Consensus" is the mean of neighbours' ratings, with agreement = 1 − standard deviation; there is no quorum rule | p. 29 |
| Recognition / inhibition | No | — |

Also in the swarm: a sensing radius r, peer-assessment range k, shaped rewards, and bounded trait updates of ±0.05 to ±0.20 (p. 29).

## 4. Baselines

- "Traditional" multi-agent system: one agent per bar under a central critic LLM that scores the full draft in [0, 1] and returns feedback (pp. 5–6, 27).
- Single-shot: one model call writes the whole piece; no repair pass (pp. 7, 30–31). The author calls it a "lower-bound baseline" (p. 30).

Explicit answers:

- Single agent with the same total token budget: **No.** The single-agent arm is one call. The two iterative arms make many calls per iteration over several iterations.
- Orchestrator / hierarchy: Yes (central critic).
- Non-bio schedule: No.
- Tokens / cost: not reported. "Compute-efficient" and "cost-efficient" are asserted relative to fine-tuning, without figures (pp. 1, 13).
- Arms matched: No. Budgets differ by construction, and the model differs between the two objectives.

On Objective #1 the central-critic system leads on unpredictability, and the text says it "slightly outperformed the swarm" on unpredictability and overall risk-taking (p. 16).

Trait dynamics: Table S1 (p. 42) fits per-trait best-response coefficients by pooled OLS, n = 192 transitions, R² from 0.359 (theme loyalty) to 0.751 (risk taking). The author states no payoff-based Nash equilibrium is claimed (p. 15).

No intervals, no p-values, no tests anywhere in the comparison.

## 6. Ablations

None. Decay, pheromones, personality diversity, memory and peer assessment are never removed. Ablations "to quantify the contribution of memory, role diversity, and peer assessment" are listed as future work (p. 24).

## 7. Scaling and robustness

Not reported. The only variation is 8 bars vs 16 bars, confounded with a different model and a different objective (pp. 7, 13).

## 8. Claims vs evidence

- Abstract: the swarm "yields superior quality" and "leads across creativity metrics" (p. 1). On Objective #1 it does not lead on unpredictability and at best ties on risk-taking (Fig. 6, p. 11). The author concedes the balance "is sensitive to evaluation method and metric definition" (p. 16).
- "Quality" for the swarm is its own peer-consensus score σ; for the other system it is the critic's score. They are not the same scale, and no external judge or listener study exists; human evaluation is named as missing (p. 24).
- Every comparison is n = 1 per arm per objective. Differences such as 0.592 vs 0.587 carry no uncertainty estimate.
- The conclusion says both agentic systems "substantially outperformed" single-shot (p. 24). Single-shot is one call with no repair, against many iterative calls. The comparison shows that iteration helps, not that swarm structure does.
- "Compute- and data-efficient" (p. 1): no compute is measured.
- The metrics reward surprise and irregularity; a noisier piece scores as more "creative". The author reports the swarm piece feels "abstract or opaque" on first listening (p. 13).

## 9. Code, data, licence

"Codes and additional materials" at https://github.com/lamm-mit/MusicSwarm (p. 32). No licence stated in the PDF text.

## 10. Verdict for our claims

- **F1 — supported.** Baselines are a central critic (hierarchy) and a single call; no single agent with matched budget, and no token or cost accounting (pp. 7, 27, 30).
- **F3 — supported.** One bio-inspired regime against two non-bio arms (Fig. 3, p. 5).
- **F5 — supported by absence.** Decay is part of the design (pp. 6, 29) but is never isolated; ablations are deferred to future work (p. 24).
