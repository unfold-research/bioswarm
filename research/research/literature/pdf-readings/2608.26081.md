# 2608.26081 — SwarmWorld

## 1. Citation as printed

Subhadeep Pal, Fiona Y. Wang, Markus J. Buehler (MIT, LAMM and other departments). "SwarmWorld: Stigmergic technological evolution in societies of language-model agents." arXiv:2608.26081v1 [cs.AI], 26 Aug 2026 (p. 1).

## 2. Task, models, agents, trials

- Task: open-ended "technological" construction in a 72 × 54 grid world (BioFoundry). Agents gather and process resources, test materials, build persistent artefacts and write controller programs. Artefacts are scored by a deterministic simulator after agents are removed, under eight unseen disturbance schedules (pp. 3–4, Fig. 3 p. 6; pp. 43–44).
- Model: gpt-5.6-luna, temperature 0.7, low reasoning effort, same prompt for all agents; 4,096 output tokens, 12 planned actions, 60,000-character retrieved context (S2.1, p. 39). One model and one prompt configuration (p. 28).
- Population study: 4 conditions × N ∈ {50, 100, 200} × 4 seeds (3201–3204) = 48 episodes, 800 ticks, 16 scheduled decisions per agent (pp. 4, 30, 43). 89,617 provider calls in total (p. 44).
- Long-horizon study: N = 100, 3 conditions × 4 new seeds (3301–3304) = 12 episodes, 3,200 ticks, 6,400 scheduled decisions per episode; checkpoints at 400, 800, 1,600, 2,400, 3,200 (pp. 4, 44).
- Transfer worlds: AshenRealm (N = 50, full communication; descriptive) (pp. 23–25) and Protein Realms pilot (N = 50, single seed 3801, 800-call budget per condition) (pp. 25, 43).
- Unit of replication is the world seed, n = 4 per cell (pp. 4, 45).

## 3. Mechanism and mapping

"Physical stigmergy": persistent, spatially located artefacts and environmental changes that later agents encounter (pp. 4, 43). On top of it, "explicit culture": messages, publications, teaching, trade, task claims, program reuse and forking (Table S2, p. 43).

## 4. Baselines

Four conditions (Fig. 3 p. 6; Table S2 p. 43): Full culture; No communication (keeps shared world and program inheritance); No explicit culture (physical stigmergy only); Independent search.

- Independent search = N isolated one-agent copies of the same seeded world; member i matches shared agent i in start position and decision phase; each endpoint is the maximum over the N members, chosen separately per endpoint and checkpoint (pp. 4, 43–44). The authors call it "a deliberately strong control" (p. 4).
- Single agent with the same total token budget: **No.** The control is N separate agents each with the per-agent budget, taken best-of-N. No arm gives one agent the whole budget.
- Orchestrator / hierarchy: No.
- Non-bio schedule: No.
- Tokens / cost: decision opportunities are matched; tokens are not. The paper states that equal opportunity "does not imply equal token use" and that token consumption is "reported separately" (p. 43). I found no token or cost figure anywhere in the PDF text.
- Arms matched: on scheduled model calls, yes (audited, p. 44). On tokens, no.

## 6. Ablations

The conditions are the ablation: remove communication; then also remove program inheritance and authored text (Table S2, p. 43). n = 4 seeds per cell, paired bootstrap. Result: the stigmergy-only condition is at least as good as full culture on inventions at every scale and checkpoint (Fig. 4E p. 7; Fig. 13F p. 15) and on held-out resilience at the end (p. 12). Full culture is ahead on best artefact from tick 800 and on portfolio from about tick 1,600 (p. 9, Fig. 13).

No ablation of decay, persistence or any threshold. Persistence of artefacts is never removed inside a shared world; the only arm without stigmergy is the isolated one, which also removes the shared population.

## 7. Scaling and robustness

- N = 50, 100, 200 (Fig. 4, p. 7). "Mechanism-dependent rather than monotonic" (p. 4). Population changes density in a fixed world (p. 43).
- Robustness is the held-out assay (agent-free, eight schedules, 288 ticks; p. 43–44).
- Network knockout (topology only): random removal of half the agents leaves 98.3% (full) and 95.2% (no explicit culture) of artefacts connected; high-degree removal 59.6% and 73.9%; broker removal 62.9% and 68.4% (p. 19). The authors note this is not physical recovery (pp. 19, 28).

## 8. Claims vs evidence

- Abstract: shared societies give "broader, more resilient technological portfolios" than best-of-N isolated search, while isolated search stays competitive for the strongest artefact (p. 1). This matches Fig. 4E and Fig. 14. The authors are careful: "not a universal swarm advantage" (p. 4).
- Small gaps. Text says portfolio resilience had positive paired effects "throughout" (p. 5); the full-culture N = 50 cell (+0.037) has no asterisk. Discovery AUC is negative at N = 50 for two shared conditions, one with interval excluding zero (−0.032*).
- All inference is on 4 seeds, one model, one prompt, simulator-defined function (stated, p. 28).
- "Outperform independent search" is against an envelope of N agents with equal calls, not equal tokens, and not against one agent with the pooled budget. Token figures promised on p. 43 are absent from the PDF.
- The comparison shared vs isolated confounds two things: persistence of other agents' artefacts, and simply having N agents' output in one world to be counted as one portfolio. Portfolio and invention counts sum over a society, while the isolated envelope is one member's world; the paper presents this as the point, but it favours the shared arm on breadth metrics by construction.
- Protein Realms: full culture and no explicit culture completed no valid assay; only no-communication and one isolated member did (p. 25, Fig. 24). Single seed.
- Three extension manifests record a dirty worktree; one N = 200 full-culture seed was rerun (p. 44).

## 10. Verdict for our claims

- **F1 — supported in the letter, weakened in spirit.** This paper does not compare with conversation or hierarchy; it compares with matched isolated search (pp. 4, 43). That is the closest thing in this set to a compute-matched non-collective control. It is still best-of-N one-agent worlds with equal calls, not one agent with the total budget, and tokens are unmatched and unreported (p. 43). Our sentence should name this paper as the exception that uses an isolated-population control.
- **F3 — supported, with a caveat.** The three shared conditions vary how much explicit culture is layered on one stigmergic substrate (Table S2, p. 43). They are not different bio-inspired regimes (no decay vs quorum vs threshold contrast).
- **F5 — not addressed.** Decay is not a studied mechanism; traces persist and nothing about their lifetime is varied (pp. 4, 43).
