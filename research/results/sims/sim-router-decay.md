## Hypothesis (preregistered before the run)
Registered in `results/sims/PREREGISTRATION.jsonl` (2026-11-11T03:16:30.972Z, v1.0.0, code sha256 `d191f23a0a3fb616…`), committed before the first run.

A router whose weights are cumulative means of acceptances readapts after an abrupt change in a time proportional to its whole history; with exponential decay (EWMA rate lambda) the time is fixed by lambda and independent of the history, at the price of a stationary loss that grows with lambda.

- **P1** — Single estimate, history T0 = 500: the number of tasks until the past weighs less than 10% is within 5% (or 2 tasks) of 4,500 without decay and of 230, 45, 22 and 11 with lambda = 0.01, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2.
- **P2** — Two-route router: the median switch time without decay grows at least 3x when the history goes from T0 = 500 to T0 = 2,000, while with lambda = 0.05 it changes by less than 25%.
- **P3** — Two-route router, T0 = 500: the median switch time without decay is at least 3x that of lambda = 0.05.
- **P4** — Stationary regime (no change, 20,000 tasks): the acceptance rate with lambda <= 0.05 is within 5% (relative) of the router without decay, and the loss with lambda = 0.2 is larger than with lambda = 0.01 (disjoint 95% CIs).

## Model
- Part A, single estimate: 500 observations at acceptance 0.8, then the true value drops to 0.4; 4,000 runs.
- Part B, two routes with acceptance 0.8 and 0.4, epsilon-greedy choice (10% exploration). After a history of T0 tasks the routes swap. Switch time = first task from which the new best route stays ahead for 100 consecutive tasks; 400 runs per cell.
- Policies: cumulative mean (no decay) and EWMA with rate 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2.
- Stationary regime: 20,000 tasks with no change, 400 runs.

## Interpretation
- **Without decay the router is as slow as its past (P2, P3).** Switch time grows 3.9× when the history grows 4×; with decay 0.05 it stays at 34–37 tasks. At T0 = 500 the gap is 15×.
- **Decay was free here (P4).** With a 0.8 vs 0.4 gap, decay up to 0.05 costs nothing measurable and 0.2 costs under 1% of acceptances. The price only appears with strong decay.
- **Formulas hold (P1).** Forgetting times match 9·T0 and ln 0.1 / ln(1 − λ).
- In the router, decay 0.2 is no faster than 0.1 (23 vs 21 tasks at T0 = 500): past a point the noise of the estimate, not its memory, sets the switch time.

## Limitations
- One abrupt change and a wide quality gap (0.8 vs 0.4). A narrow gap (for example 0.6 vs 0.5) raises the stationary price of decay and was not preregistered here.
- Two routes; acceptance is a clean binary signal. Real acceptance signals are delayed and noisy.
- **What needs validation with real agents:** how often route quality really changes (model updates, task mix) and the noise of the acceptance signal in Void and Murmur.
