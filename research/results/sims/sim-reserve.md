## Hypothesis (preregistered before the run)
Registered in `results/sims/PREREGISTRATION.jsonl` (2026-11-11T03:16:31.022Z, v1.0.0, code sha256 `531f6cf3cf340ceb…`), committed before the first run.

Near saturation the waiting time of a task queue is strongly nonlinear in the number of agents, so a small idle reserve cuts latency under bursts and failures far more than it costs; at low utilization the same reserve buys almost nothing.

- **P1** — Steady load 3.6 (service rate 1 per agent): the simulated mean wait for c = 4, 5 and 6 is within 10% of the Erlang C value (about 1.97, 0.29 and 0.08).
- **P2** — Bursts (load 4.5 during 20% of the time, 3.6 otherwise): one reserve agent (c = 5) cuts the p95 wait of c = 4 by at least 60%, and two (c = 6) by at least 85%.
- **P3** — Low utilization (load 1.8, c = 4, utilization 0.45): the mean-wait reduction from one reserve agent is less than 5% of the reduction obtained at load 3.6 (utilization 0.9).
- **P4** — Stuck agents (each fails at rate 0.002 and recovers after a mean of 20, load 3.6): the p95 wait of c = 4 is at least 2x its value without failures, and one reserve agent brings it below the no-failure c = 4 value.

## Model
- M/M/c queue simulated event by event: Poisson arrivals, exponential service at rate 1 per agent, FIFO; 100 runs of 20,000 time units per cell after 1,000 of warm-up.
- Steady: load 3.6. Low: load 1.8. Bursts: load 4.5 during periods of mean length 50, load 3.6 during periods of mean length 200. Failures: each working agent fails at rate 0.002 and returns after a mean of 20; its task goes back to the head of the queue.
- Agents: 4 (no reserve), 5 and 6.

## Interpretation
- **The simulator reproduces Erlang C (P1)**, so the remaining numbers can be read as queueing results.
- **Near saturation one reserve agent changes the regime (P2, P4).** Under bursts the p95 wait drops 92% with one extra agent and 97.5% with two; with stuck agents, four agents wait 3.4× longer at p95 than without failures and a fifth brings the wait below the no-failure level.
- **At low utilization the reserve is only cost (P3).** The same extra agent reduces the mean wait by 0.046 at 45% utilization against 1.69 at 90%.

## Limitations
- Poisson arrivals and exponential service; heavy-tailed task durations and correlated failures make waits worse than shown.
- An idle agent is assumed free to keep and instantly available; warm-up or context-loading cost is not modelled.
- **What needs validation with real agents:** real arrival and duration distributions of tasks in Void, and the cost of keeping a warm agent idle.
