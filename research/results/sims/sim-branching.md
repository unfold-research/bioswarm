## Hypothesis (preregistered before the run)
Registered in `results/sims/PREREGISTRATION.jsonl` (2026-11-11T03:16:30.921Z, v1.0.0, code sha256 `f5db9298d7527834…`), committed before the first run.

When every executed job spawns m children on average and a fraction d is cancelled before running, work per root job is 1/(1 - m(1-d)) below m_ef = m(1-d) = 1 and unbounded at or above it; the transition is abrupt, the minimum cleanup is d* = 1 - 1/m, and a hard depth limit bounds the work at a cost that grows geometrically with the depth.

- **P1** — Subcritical, no cleanup: for m = 0.8, 0.9 and 0.95 the mean number of executed jobs per root is within 3% of 1/(1-m) (5, 10, 20) and no root reaches the 20,000-job cap.
- **P2** — Abrupt threshold: the fraction of roots that reach the cap is < 0.1% for m_ef in {0.8, 0.9, 0.95} and > 5% for m_ef in {1.05, 1.2, 1.5}.
- **P3** — Critical cleanup: with m = 1.5, cleanup d = 0.30 (m_ef = 1.05) still has > 5% of roots reaching the cap, while d = 0.40 (m_ef = 0.9) has < 0.1% and a mean of executed jobs per root within 3% of 10.
- **P4** — Backlog after arrivals stop (service capacity 50 jobs/step, 5 roots/step for 200 steps): the backlog is empty 1,000 steps later in >= 99% of runs for m_ef in {0.8, 0.95}, and is larger than at the stop in >= 90% of runs for m_ef in {1.05, 1.2}.
- **P5** — Depth limit: with m = 1.5 and no cleanup, a maximum depth D bounds the mean executed jobs per root within 3% of (m^(D+1) - 1)/(m - 1) for D in {3, 5, 8} (8.1, 20.8, 74.9).

## Model
- Each executed job spawns Poisson(m) children; each child is cancelled or deduplicated with probability d before it runs, so m_ef = m(1 − d).
- 200,000 root jobs per cell; a root that reaches 20,000 executed jobs counts as diverged.
- Backlog model: 50 jobs served per step, 5 root jobs arriving per step for 200 steps, then 1,000 steps with no arrivals; 500 runs per cell.
- Depth limit: children deeper than D generations are never spawned.

## Interpretation
- **The threshold is abrupt (P1, P2).** Below m_ef = 1 no root in 200,000 diverged and the mean matches 1/(1 − m_ef); at 1.05 about 9% diverge, at 1.5 about 58%. There is no gradual region to tune inside.
- **Cleanup has a minimum (P3).** With m = 1.5 the critical cleanup is d* = 33%: 30% still diverges, 40% is stable at 10 jobs per root. Exactly at the threshold (d = 0.3334) a few roots still reach the cap, so the practical target sits clearly above d*.
- **The backlog tells the two regimes apart only after arrivals stop (P4).** While roots arrive, both regimes accumulate work; afterwards the subcritical backlog drains to zero in every run and the supercritical one keeps growing.
- **A depth limit is a bound, not a cure (P5).** It makes the work finite, but each extra level multiplies the cost by m (8 → 21 → 75 jobs per root for D = 3, 5, 8).

## Limitations
- Children are independent and identically distributed; real subtask counts depend on the task and on the agent that spawned them.
- These are analytic results (Galton–Watson) confirmed by Monte Carlo: they validate the simulator and the formula, not any LLM system.
- **What needs validation with real agents:** the measured children per job (m̂) and cancelled fraction (d̂) per task type in Void and Murmur traces, and whether deduplication behaves like independent cancellation.
