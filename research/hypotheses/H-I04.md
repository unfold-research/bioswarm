# Instincts enter through the operator channel, not from peers

**Status: testing. Formal adoption was zero on both models; on Haiku the note changed the decision anyway (2026-11-11).** Proposed engineering hypothesis of the instinct family (`programs/historical/instincts.md`). No experiment has run.

## Statement
An instinct offered by another agent inside the work stream is not adopted; the same instinct installed by the operator is. Exchange between agents therefore needs an explicit, gated channel.

## Variable
channel that delivers the instinct: operator installation / note from a peer agent in the input

## Outcomes
adoption rate per channel (the instinct fires where it should)

## Prediction
adoption through a peer note stays at or below two cases in eight while operator installation reaches at least six

## Refutation criterion
peer notes are adopted as often as operator installation

## Strong conventional baseline
no exchange: every agent keeps only what its operator wrote

## Basis in this repository
B1 (an injected order from a quoted source had no effect in 12 of 12 cases). These motivate the hypothesis; they do not test it.

## Result (2026-11-11, Claude Sonnet 5.5)
`results/batch2-r1-sonnet/REPORT.md`: the card arriving as a note from another agent was adopted in 0 of 24 exposed cases; installed by the operator it fired in 39 of 40 (native 24 of 24, transplant 15 of 16). One phrasing of the peer note; I3 tests stronger ones.

## Result (I3, 2026-11-11, Claude Haiku 5.5)
`results/batch2-i3-peer-pressure/REPORT.md`: adoption was 0 of 32 exposed cases across four strengths of peer pressure, including a claim of operator approval, three agreeing agents and an imitation of an operator message.

## Result (I1, 2026-11-11, Claude Haiku 5.5)
`results/batch2-i1-transplant/REPORT.md`: a peer note was never formally adopted (0 of 24 quarantines; 0 of 32 in I3), but the verdict followed the note's instinct in 30 of 56 exposed cases across I1 and I3, against 3 of 24 with no note. On Sonnet 5.5 it was 0 of 24. The operator channel worked on both models. The hypothesis as worded ("is not adopted") holds for declarations and fails for decisions on the smaller model.

## Evaluation scope and limits
Synthetic tasks in owned or isolated environments, at matched model budget. Cases, seeds, stopping rules and a spending cap are preregistered before any paid run.

## Result (I4, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-i4-covert/REPORT.md`: formal adoption from a peer note 0 of 96. The registered test of covert uptake was not supported on Haiku (uplift 8, 2 and 0 of 16 in triage, research and review) and refuted on Sonnet. The effect reported after I1 and I3 holds in the triage family only. Unregistered observation: a note proposing a rule suppressed spontaneous quarantine in the review family on both models (8 and 10 of 16 down to 0).

## Result (I6, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-i6-advice/REPORT.md`: any rule placed in the context, by a peer or by the operator, removed the quarantine both models do unprompted (9 and 11 of 16 down to 0); a note with no rule halved it. The channel matters less than expected: the first installed rule of any origin changes unprompted behaviour.

## Result (I9, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-i9-rules/REPORT.md`: an installed instinct removes unprompted quarantine (10 and 9 of 16 to 0); a plain operator sentence does not; a sentence saying instincts do not replace judgement does not restore it (0 and 0).

## Result (I10, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-i10-explicit/REPORT.md`: the unprompted behaviour (8 of 16) written down as an instinct fired on 16 of 16 and stayed at 16 with other instincts installed before or after it, on both models.

## Result (C1 and S3, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-c1-typed/REPORT.md`: on Sonnet the same facts lowered unprompted quarantine as a free-text note (8 to 2 of 16) and not as typed traces (9 and 12 of 16); on both models typed traces raised it above the baseline. Haiku inconclusive as registered (baseline 0.28). `results/batch2-s3-trigger/REPORT.md`: a card injected by the harness only where its trigger holds leaves the baseline intact on other cases (7 and 8 of 16 against 0 with the card always present) and removes unprompted behaviour on the cases where it fires (6 and 5 of 16 to 0).

## Result (C1b, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-c1b-label/REPORT.md`: the same three facts raised unprompted quarantine as traces from another agent (+0.22 and +0.25) and, on Haiku, as an unattributed block (0.50 to 0.88), and left it unchanged as a status board kept by the operator (0.53 against 0.50). On Haiku one sentence saying traces are untrusted, with no traces present, lowered it from 0.50 to 0.16.
