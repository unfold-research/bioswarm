# Instincts interfere beyond a small number

**Status: supported for colliding triggers (I5), not for disjoint ones (I2).** Proposed engineering hypothesis of the instinct family (`programs/historical/instincts.md`).

## Statement
Each added instinct lowers the reliability of the others once an agent holds more than a handful, so a population needs selection, not accumulation.

## Variable
number of instincts installed at once: 1, 3, 6, 12, with unrelated triggers

## Outcomes
effect of a target instinct, wrong firings, as a function of the number installed

## Prediction
the effect of the target instinct drops by at least two cases in eight between 1 and 12 installed instincts

## Refutation criterion
no drop up to 12

## Strong conventional baseline
one consolidated policy document

## Basis in this repository
H-23 (sparse activation), H-24 (growth without cleanup). These motivate the hypothesis; they do not test it.

## Result (I2, 2026-11-11, Claude Haiku 5.5)
`results/batch2-i2-interference/REPORT.md`: the effect of the target instinct was 6, 6, 6 and 5 of 8 with 1, 3, 6 and 12 instincts installed, with no wrong firing. The other instincts had disjoint, inert triggers; competing triggers were not tested.

## Evaluation scope and limits
Synthetic tasks in owned or isolated environments, at matched model budget. Cases, seeds, stopping rules and a spending cap are preregistered before any paid run.

## Result (I5, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-i5-rivals/REPORT.md`: with a rival instinct on the same trigger the effect of the target fell from 12 to 3 and 0 of 16 on Haiku and from 16 to 4 and 4 on Sonnet, in either order. An explicit priority was followed by Sonnet (16 and 3) and not by Haiku (6 and 5). Interference comes from overlapping triggers, not from the number installed.

## Result (I7, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-i7-overlap/REPORT.md`: interference stays inside the overlap of the triggers on both models. A broader escalating instinct removed the target on Haiku (13 to 0) and not on Sonnet (16 to 16). An exclusion clause written at admission restored the target on Sonnet (16) and not on Haiku (4).

## Result (I8, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-i8-merge/REPORT.md`: the collision replicated (12 to 6 and 16 to 2 of 16) and disappeared when the two instincts were merged into one card (11 and 16), when the rival was refused (12 and 16), and when one card carried both actions under a condition (14 and 16 of 16 right).

## Result (I11, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-i11-admission/REPORT.md`: an admission agent detected every collision. Its written resolution worked for an identical trigger and failed for a narrower one on both admission models: Haiku wrote a card against the policy; Sonnet wrote a correct card the small executor did not follow.

## Result (I12, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-i12-gate/REPORT.md`: inconclusive as registered (the installed instinct alone fired on 11 of 16, one below the required 12). Descriptively, a card template repaired the merge of a broader newcomer (13, 14, 13 of 16 where a free-form card gave 1) and not of a narrower one (8, 8, 10 and 8, 8, 13); the newcomer's action outside the collision was kept in 1 or 2 of 8 cases. A remark here that the I8 branch result was in doubt was tested in I13 and withdrawn.

## Result (I13, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-i13-branch/REPORT.md`: a card with two mutually exclusive conditions was followed in both directions by Haiku (62 of 64 branch decisions) and by Sonnet except for 3 of 12 cases on one branch. The merged cards that failed in I11 and I12 expressed precedence between overlapping conditions, which the small model does not apply.

## Result (I14, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-11)
`results/batch2-i14-partition/REPORT.md`: with merged cards written as partitions, a regression gate admitted the rival and broad sets and refused all six narrow ones on both admission models; on held-out cases 1 of 12 admitted sets slipped. The newcomer's action outside the collision was kept in 12 of 48 cases.
