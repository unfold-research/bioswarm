# A transplanted instinct keeps its effect

**Status: preliminary-support (Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5, 2026-11-11).** Proposed engineering hypothesis of the instinct family (`programs/historical/instincts.md`). No experiment has run.

## Statement
An instinct written and measured in one agent population keeps most of its effect when installed unchanged in agents doing a different kind of work with the same underlying situation.

## Variable
recipient population: same task family as the donor / different task family; form: native wording / donor wording unchanged

## Outcomes
effect in the recipient relative to a natively written instinct in the same family; wrong firings

## Prediction
the transplant keeps at least 80% of the native effect in each recipient family, with no more wrong firings

## Refutation criterion
the transplant keeps less than half of the native effect in a recipient family, or fires wrongly in two or more cases in eight

## Strong conventional baseline
rewriting the rule by hand for each population

## Basis in this repository
none yet: no experiment in this repository tests transfer. These motivate the hypothesis; they do not test it.

## Result (2026-11-11, Claude Sonnet 5.5)
`results/batch2-r1-sonnet/REPORT.md`: the donor instinct card, unchanged, kept 7 of 8 (research) and 8 of 8 (review) of the native effect of 8 of 8, with no wrong firing. Near transfer between parallel vocabularies; eight cases per cell.

## Result (I1, 2026-11-11, Claude Haiku 5.5)
`results/batch2-i1-transplant/REPORT.md`: the donor card, unchanged, fired in 8 of 8 exposed cases in both recipient families, equal to the native wording, with no wrong firing.

## Evaluation scope and limits
Synthetic tasks in owned or isolated environments, at matched model budget. Cases, seeds, stopping rules and a spending cap are preregistered before any paid run.
